## Selection standard

This shortlist replaces weak directory-derived names for the next research/outreach wave. It is a **qualified research shortlist**, not a claim that every firm has agreed to participate or that every firm is an SME. No outreach has been sent and no owner approval to contact is recorded.

Selection prioritises customs brokers, freight forwarders and logistics providers that publicly describe customs-clearance, customs-brokerage, import/export declaration or customs-documentation services. Those workflows make invoice/document intake relevant to a discovery conversation.

**Register caveats**
- Canada: the CBSA official page is titled “Licensed customs brokers” and publishes firm names, websites and in many cases email addresses. Inclusion below is direct evidence of appearing on that list.
- Australia: the Australian Border Force brokerage list says it helps identify customs brokers that may be authorised, but also says some brokers decline inclusion and the published list is current as of 14 March 2025. Inclusion is a register/list match, not a fresh 2026 licence-status certificate.
- United Kingdom: HMRC's customs-agent register is updated 15 September 2026, but HMRC expressly says entries are supplied by the businesses and are not vetted, approved, recommended or guaranteed current. This research round used current company service pages and official Companies House / company records where found; do not describe these firms as HMRC-approved based on the directory.

## Why these accounts are not all equal

- **Best first discovery targets:** independent / specialist Canadian brokers (Clear, Noble, Ambassador, A & A, Buckland, Pacific) because they are directly in the document workflow and the regulator's list supplies a business contact route.
- **Second tier:** Australian brokers listed by ABF; current contact and service scope must be checked because the public list is dated March 2025 and does not provide contact details.
- **Enterprise / longer-cycle:** UK and multinational forwarders (DSV, Kuehne+Nagel, DHL, Rhenus, CEVA). They are relevant but may have longer procurement/security reviews and existing software stacks. Barrington Freight remains conditional until customs-document services are confirmed.

## Mandatory pre-outreach checks

1. Confirm each account's official website and current contact route; for Australian firms, confirm the current legal entity and customs-broker status because the ABF list is dated.
2. Confirm target customer fit: recurring invoice volume, manual rekeying or checking pain, and willingness to test an external tool.
3. Confirm whether they process supplier/commercial invoices in a way Quillvora can safely support; do not assume the product's extraction is validated for their documents.
4. For UK enterprise firms, locate the right operations / customs compliance decision-maker; a generic contact form is not a qualified lead.
5. Keep `owner_approval_to_contact=no` until the owner approves the batch. No outreach is authorised by this document.

## Product claims to avoid

Do not claim proven accuracy, time savings, compliance certification, production readiness for customer data, or customer results. The existing demo uses synthetic invoices only; customer interviews and real-world accuracy testing have not yet occurred.
